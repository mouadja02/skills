# Working forms

Copy and adapt these forms to the task. Leave unknown fields marked as unknown; omit fields that do not apply. A completed form is a record of work performed, not evidence that an unperformed check occurred.

## Research brief and handoff

- **Request and decision:** What must the reader learn or decide? Who is the audience?
- **Question and bounds:** Population, place, period, concepts, comparison, and outcome where relevant.
- **Approach:** Mode, sources available, search terms and dates, selection criteria, and constraints.
- **Answer:** Direct response and confidence, with references next to important claims.
- **Evidence:** For each key finding, list its source, relevant result, context, and limitations. Identify disagreements and possible explanations.
- **Interpretation:** Separate observations from inference and recommendations; name plausible alternatives and evidence gaps.
- **Handoff:** Provide a bibliography with stable links or identifiers when known, source access status, search record, open questions, and suggested next actions. State what was not checked. Include the user's requested format or citation style where practical.

For an offline task, work from materials actually supplied or locally available. Record their origin and date if known. Label external claims and citations that cannot be checked, and distinguish a document's assertions from independently verified findings. Do not imply a live search or current coverage.

## Review protocol

Complete before screening when possible; date revisions and explain deviations.

- **Question and rationale:** Population/setting, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcomes, and study designs as applicable.
- **Eligibility:** Inclusion and exclusion rules, languages, dates, publication types, and reasons for each boundary.
- **Search:** Sources and access method; full search strings and filters per source; dates searched; additional citation searches; known coverage gaps.
- **Selection:** Deduplication method; screening stages, reviewers, disagreement resolution, and exclusion reasons. Identify any single-reviewer stages.
- **Extraction:** Fields to collect, procedures for missing or conflicting information, and checks on extraction.
- **Appraisal:** Design-appropriate risk-of-bias tool and decision rules; assessor arrangements and limitations.
- **Synthesis:** Planned grouping, effect measures, conditions for pooling, heterogeneity and sensitivity checks, or narrative synthesis approach; treatment of missing data and confidence in evidence.
- **Registration and changes:** Registry and identifier only if registration actually occurred; protocol versions, deviations, and rationale.

## Evidence and screening ledger

Maintain one row per record during screening and one row per study/outcome as needed during extraction. Preserve identifiers that link multiple reports of the same study.

| ID / citation / link | Source and search date | Stage and decision | Exclusion reason | Study design and setting | Sample / intervention / comparator | Outcome, result and uncertainty | Appraisal / limitations | Notes or missing data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | |

Record counts for identified records, duplicates removed, records screened, full texts assessed, exclusions by reason, and studies included. Reconcile counts and disclose unavailable full texts and uncompleted steps. If using a reporting checklist, identify the version and report only what was actually done.

## Monitoring plan

Only set up alerts or recurring work when requested and supported. Agree the question, sources, query, update frequency, recipient or destination, and end or review date. Define triggers for inclusion, corrections or retractions, and contradictory findings. Keep a dated record of each check and changes to previous conclusions. If monitoring cannot be activated, provide a manual procedure and clearly label it inactive.
